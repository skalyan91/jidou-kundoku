rongo-gakuji.conllu is a derived work, and this notice is the ShareAlike
condition met: it too is CC BY-SA 4.0. It was built by
scripts/build-analects-sample.py, which takes the treebank's own sentences for
the whole of 論語 卷一 (學而第一) — sent_id prefix KR1h0004_001, 82 sentences
and 499 Han characters — verbatim, in reading order, and changes the following
and nothing else. The script prints every one of them on each run.

  1. Ten pairs of adjacent punctuation marks are put in the order the received
     text writes them: 乎？」 for 乎」？ eight times, 子曰：「 for 子曰「： once,
     and 曰：「《詩》 for 曰：《「詩》 once. Each pair swaps whole, so every mark
     keeps the token it hangs off; no mark is added or removed.

  2. The first token of each of the sixteen chapters is given LineBreak=para,
     so the panels set the chapters as paragraphs and the 篇 heading stands
     alone as one.

  3. The 篇 heading 學而篇第一 is given its on'yomi — がく・じ・へん・だい・いち,
     pinned per character in MISC under Reading — and its 學 is retagged from
     VERB to NOUN. Without the retag the reading is conjugated and the heading
     comes out 學びて篇第一, a sentence made of a title. This is the only
     part-of-speech change in the file.

  4. 為政篇第二, the *next* juan's heading, which the treebank's merge left
     inside 學而's closing sentence, is cut away and the remaining 患不知人也
     re-rooted on 患. This is a truncation, not a correction: 為政第二 is
     outside the extract, as chapter 17 would be.

Apart from (3) and the re-rooting that (4) forces, no dependency arc is
altered and no content token is moved.

The underlying text of the 論語 is some two and a half millennia old and is in
the public domain; what CC BY-SA covers here is the treebank's annotation of it
— the segmentation, the parts of speech, the morphological features and the
dependency trees.

── On the punctuation and readings, and what is deliberately absent ──

Two outside sources for the received edition's punctuation were sought, and
neither could be used. Everything in this file is the treebank's own, ordered
as described above; the readings are the app's own resolver's, except the five
heading characters named in (3).

**kanbun.info** (Web漢文大系, 藤川全祐) was asked for its punctuation and its
書き下し文. The site publishes no terms of use, no licence and no permissions
page. The one permission it grants is to link to it:

  "当サイトは、どのページにリンクしていただいてもかまいませんし、その際の
   ご連絡等も必要ありません。"
  (You may link to any page on this site, and no notification is required.)
    https://kanbun.info/about/about.html

Every page carries "© Web Kanbun-Taikei", and captures of the same page from
2005 and 2012 read "All rights reserved" in full. The 書き下し文 is the
editorial work of a living author — the site's own 凡例 argues its choice of
readings at length — and under Japanese copyright law silence about reuse is
not permission. Bundling an extract here would further purport to sublicense
that work to everyone downstream under this repository's terms, which no
quotation right permits. So nothing of it is here. Permission could be asked
for at the address the site publishes for corrections.

**The Kanseki Repository** (漢籍リポジトリ) was asked first, and is out on
different grounds. Its KR1h0004 is the same 論語 the treebank's sent_ids name,
so the two are editions of one text — and none of it is here either. Kanripo's
site footer licenses "all content created by us"
CC BY-SA 4.0, but KR1h0004's own file header declares BASEEDITION CHANT /
WITNESS chant, and the Kanripo catalogue names 阮元's 1816 江西南昌府學
recutting of the Song 《論語注疏》 as its base: the transcription is the CHinese
ANcient Texts database's (漢達文庫, D. C. Lau Research Centre for Chinese
Ancient Texts, Institute of Chinese Studies, CUHK), which is not Kanripo's to
license onward. CHANT's own agreement reads:

  "No part of the Site shall be reproduced or adapted without prior written
   permission approved by CUHK."

Merging its punctuation into another edition is an adaptation. With two
incompatible claims resting on the same bytes, nothing was taken: no character
of Kanripo's or CHANT's text is in this repository, and the marks in
rongo-gakuji.conllu are the treebank's own, reordered as described above.

── The other two samples ──

shuchu.conllu (聊齋志異・酒蟲) is not covered by this notice. Its text is Pu
Songling's, long out of copyright, and its annotation was made by hand in this
app; it carries no third-party data.

shunbou.conllu (杜甫・春望) is not covered by it either, and the distinction is
worth writing down because the treebank was consulted for it.

  The text is Du Fu's, of 757, and out of copyright by any measure.

  The annotation is the shipped parser's own output over those forty
  characters — lzh_sud_kyoto, the wheel in public/wasm/wheels/ — corrected by
  hand. No sentence, no tree and no token of the Kyoto treebank is in the file.
  What was taken from it is *counts*: how often the gold tags 更 as ADV rather
  than VERB, whether it writes 烽火 as a coordination or as a modifier, whether
  it ever puts VerbForm=Conv on a VERB. Those are measurements over a corpus,
  not the corpus, and each one is recorded in the file's own comments beside
  the decision it settled. A frequency is a fact about the data and not a
  reproduction of it, so the ShareAlike condition is not engaged; the treebank
  is named here anyway, because a reader ought to be able to check the numbers.

  Nothing of kanbun.info is in it. Its 書き下し文 of this poem was used the way
  the 3,419-passage comparison corpus is used and on the same terms — as a
  target to measure against, held locally and not redistributed. Where the
  test suite quotes a line of 春望's reading it quotes the *received* reading,
  which is the traditional kundoku of one of the best-known poems in the
  Japanese school canon and is no editor's private work; the quotations are
  short, attributed, and there to say what the app is being checked against,
  which is the purpose 引用 exists for.
